Agenda

Four parts, one operating model.

Section 01

FrameThe rules and the milestones.

Operating standard

Six rules that make the model auditable.

Each rule names an artifact someone can retrieve.

01

Every requirement is traceable

Linked to its ticket, pull request, test cases, defects, and the release that carries it.

02

Every task has an owner

A named person, a due date, and a backup owner for every critical module.

03

Every change has evidence

A ticket, acceptance criteria, a reviewed pull request, and test evidence.

04

Every release has approval

QA sign-off, UAT where needed, a security check, and a named deployment approver.

05

Every incident has a resolution path

Severity, an owner, a communication rhythm, and a record of how service was restored.

06

Every recurring problem has a corrective action

A problem record with an owner and evidence that the cause was removed.

Delivery milestones

Four milestones. Reported separately.

A feature counts as delivered to the business only at the last milestone.

  1. Milestone 01

    Development complete

    Merged through review, a linked ticket, and the quality gates.

  2. Milestone 02

    QA complete

    Tests, regression, and retests done. Release blockers closed or formally accepted.

  3. Milestone 03

    UAT approved

    The named business owner accepted the scenarios, with exceptions written down.

  4. Milestone 04 · Delivered

    Released to production

    Deployed, and the critical business journeys validated afterwards.

Section 02

BuildWhere to start, the controls, and the register.

Where to start

Five deliverables before the rest.

Put these into daily use first. The rest of the handbook follows once this core is consistent.

  1. 01Master project tracker & management dashboard

    One source of truth for all three platforms. Every item has an owner, module, priority, status, sprint, due date, and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02Engineering development & pull request SOP

    Definition of Ready, branch naming, mandatory review, linked tickets, test evidence, quality gates, and protected branches.

  3. 03QA, UAT & release management SOP

    Entry and exit criteria, regression, business sign-off, and a release checklist that ends with journey validation.

  4. 04Production support & incident management SOP

    Monitoring, the support module, Jira, email, severity, stakeholder updates, and a path to corrective action.

  5. 05Business requirement & product configuration SOP

    Intake, analysis, acceptance criteria, named approval, configuration testing, versioning, and traceability.

Completion tests

A step is finished only when the evidence exists.

Four questions a lead can ask in the daily sync without opening every SOP.

Work

Ticket and evidence

No work starts without a ticket. No ticket closes without acceptance criteria and the required evidence.

Release

Journeys validated

The release closes when critical business journeys have been checked in production, after the deployment.

Recovery

Restore demonstrated

A drill has restored the service and verified data integrity within the agreed RTO and RPO.

Configuration

Human authorization

AI-drafted formulas, rules, and documents are published only after validation and a named approval.

Release management

Ten gates, in order.

Rollback and database recovery are decided before the window opens. An emergency hotfix still needs testing and a retrospective approval.

  1. 01Scope and version freeze
  2. 02Code freeze and merge approval
  3. 03QA sign-off and regression
  4. 04UAT sign-off where required
  5. 05Security and configuration checks
  6. 06Database migration and backup
  7. 07Deployment approval and window
  8. 08Execution by a named owner
  9. 09Release completeSmoke test and business validation
  10. 10Monitor, close, or roll back

Production support

One incident path for all three platforms.

P&C runs it through the Response Center. Salik–LIVA and Motor use the same severity, ownership, and communication rules.

  1. 01 · DetectAPM, logs, uptime, API failures, and business alerts
  2. 02 · RecordTicket, logs, environment, correlation ID, and product
  3. 03 · ClassifySeverity, impact, module team, and a commander when major
  4. 04 · RecoverInvestigate, mitigate, communicate, fix, and validate
  5. 05 · PreventRoot cause, corrective action, and a post-incident review

Every incident record carries

  • Platform
  • Environment
  • Service
  • Business impact
  • Severity
  • First detected
  • Ticket owner
  • Timeline
  • Linked deployment
  • Root cause
  • Corrective action
  • Next update time

AI-assisted product configuration

AI drafts. People approve.

Drafts come from an approved specification. Publication stays with a product owner or an authorized underwriter.

  1. 01Draft fields and validation
  2. 02Draft formulas, rules, and documents
  3. 03Consistency checks
  4. 04Scenario validation
  5. 05GateHuman approval
  6. 06Version, publish, and audit
  7. 07Monitor and roll back

Formulas, underwriting rules, and customer-facing documents are never published automatically. The same rule covers Motor data cleansing before a vehicle is rated.

Management dashboard

One scorecard. Three platforms.

Drill down by platform, module, sprint, and owner. Same definitions everywhere. Baseline first, then targets.

01

Delivery

Commit versus done. Sprint predictability, milestones, and release status.

02

Quality

Defect trends, escaped defects, reopen rate, and regression results.

03

Reliability

Availability, latency, incidents, and time to restore service.

04

Resources

Allocation, support load, blockers, leave, and module ownership.

Monthly KPIs

Ten measures, one definition each.

Sprint completion rate

Planned versus completed work.

Cycle time

From the start of work to completion.

Release frequency

How often production releases occur.

Change failure rate

Deployments needing rollback, hotfix, or remediation.

Defect escape rate

Defects found after release, against all defects.

MTTR

Mean time to restore service after an incident.

SLA compliance

Incidents handled inside agreed targets.

API success rate

Reliability of critical external integrations.

Reopened ticket rate

Gaps in implementation or verification.

Overdue action items

Decisions and actions past their due date.

Judge contribution through quality, predictability, and collaboration. Account for complexity, support duty, review time, and dependencies.

Monthly management pack

Nine source reports. Four packs.

Business performance, delivery, reliability, and people stay distinct in the monthly review.

Pack 1 · SOP-001

Business transaction metrics

What volume did each platform process, how did it move, and where did conversion or transactions fail?

Pack 2 · SOP-002, 006, 008

Delivery & release

What reached production and UAT, what is pending, and what is committed next month?

Pack 3 · SOP-003, 005, 007, 009

Technology & reliability

How are predictability, quality, deployments, incidents, API reliability, and recovery trending?

Pack 4 · SOP-004, 006

Team & next month

What did each person and module deliver, and what are next month’s sprint commitments?

Pack 1 · Business transaction metrics

Define the unit before the number.

Unique quotes apart from quote versions. API attempts apart from successful transactions. New policies apart from renewals and endorsements.

Transaction volume

  • Initiated and completed
  • Quotes and policies issued
  • Premium or transaction value
  • By product, insurer, broker, channel

Conversion

  • Leads, quotes, and policies
  • Quote-to-policy conversion
  • Drop-off by journey stage
  • Time from quote to issue

Operational performance

  • Successful and failed transactions
  • Failure rate and top reasons
  • External API success and downtime
  • Retries and reconciliation gaps

Month on month

  • Current month against the last
  • Absolute change and growth
  • Product and platform trends
  • Reasons for a material move

Section 04

HoldMake it enforceable, then roll it out.

Enforceability

Every SOP uses the same twelve parts.

  1. 01SOP ID, title, and version
  2. 02Purpose and scope
  3. 03Applicable platforms and modules
  4. 04Process owner and roles
  5. 05Trigger and required inputs
  6. 06Step-by-step procedure
  7. 07Checklist and approval gates
  8. 08Expected outputs and evidence
  9. 09SLA, deadlines, and escalation
  10. 10Exceptions and emergencies
  11. 11Tools, templates, and links
  12. 12Audit trail and change history

Ownership

Named approver. An audit trail.

R

Responsible

Does the work and produces the evidence.

A

Approver

Signs the output and accepts exceptions.

C

Consulted

Contributes before the decision is made.

I

Informed

Receives the outcome and any change in date.

  • Central repositoryVersion-controlled home for every SOP
  • Master registerOwner and next review date per SOP
  • AcknowledgmentTraining or sign-off for the people it applies to
  • Compliance auditPeriodic check of evidence against procedure
  • Exception registerApproved deviations, with expiry and approver
  • Corrective actionRaised when the same gap shows up again

Rollout

Four phases. About six weeks.

Week 1

Foundation

  • Appoint process owners
  • Standardize the master tracker
  • Agree ticket statuses
  • Start the daily lead sync
  • Define the monthly pack
Weeks 2–3

Engineering quality

  • Branching and pull request rules
  • QA entry and exit criteria
  • Regression for critical journeys
  • Requirement templates
  • Release checklist
Weeks 4–5

Reliability & security

  • Alert routing
  • Support ticket automation
  • Incident SLAs
  • Security gates
  • Backup verification
  • Recovery runbooks
Week 6

Governance & audit

  • Review compliance evidence
  • Measure the first KPI baseline
  • Record gaps and exceptions
  • Refine SOPs that failed audit
  • Publish the handbook
