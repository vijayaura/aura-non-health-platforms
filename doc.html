02 · Operating standard

Six rules that make the model auditable.

Each rule names an artifact someone can retrieve: a trace from requirement to release, a tracker field, a pull request, an approval, an incident record, or a corrective action.

  1. 01

    Every requirement is traceable

    A requirement links to its development ticket, pull request, test cases, defects, and the release that carries it.

  2. 02

    Every task has an owner

    A named person on the tracker, a due date, and a backup owner for every critical module. Sprint commitment includes that owner.

  3. 03

    Every change has evidence

    A ticket, acceptance criteria, a reviewed pull request, test evidence, and a link from the requirement through to the release.

  4. 04

    Every release has approval

    QA sign-off, UAT where the change needs it, a security check, and a named deployment approver. Exceptions are written down before the window.

  5. 05

    Every incident has a resolution path

    Severity, an owner, a communication rhythm, and a record of how service was restored. The same path is used on all three platforms.

  6. 06

    Every recurring problem has a corrective action

    Repeated incidents move to a problem record with an owner and a due date. Closure requires evidence that the cause was removed.

03 · Delivery milestones

Four milestones. Report each one on its own.

Monthly updates separate completed development, completed QA, UAT approval, and production release. A feature counts as delivered to the business only at the last of these.

  1. Milestone 01

    Development complete

    Code has merged through the required review, linked ticket, and quality gates.

  2. Milestone 02

    QA complete

    Planned tests, regression, and retests are done. Release-blocking defects are closed or formally accepted.

  3. Milestone 03

    UAT approved

    The named business owner has accepted the scenarios, with any exception written down.

  4. Milestone 04

    Released to production

    The deployment finished and the critical business journeys were validated afterwards.

04 · Where to start

Five deliverables before the rest of the handbook.

Put these into daily use first. The remaining security, recovery, architecture, people, and improvement SOPs land once this core is consistent.

  1. 01

    Master project tracker & management dashboard

    One source of truth for P&C, Salik–LIVA, and Motor. Every item has an owner, platform, module, priority, status, sprint, due date, and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02

    Engineering development & pull request SOP

    Definition of Ready, branch naming, mandatory review, linked tickets, test evidence, quality gates, and protected production branches.

  3. 03

    QA, UAT & release management SOP

    Entry and exit criteria, regression, business sign-off, and a release checklist that ends with journey validation.

  4. 04

    Production support & incident management SOP

    Monitoring, the support module, Jira, email, severity, stakeholder updates, and a path from incident to corrective action.

  5. 05

    Business requirement & product configuration SOP

    Intake, analysis, acceptance criteria, named approval, configuration testing, versioning, and traceability through to release.

Security gates, disaster recovery drills, architecture review, ownership and handover, and SOP compliance audits follow in the same handbook. They are part of the register from day one and become operating routine after the five above are stable.

05 · Completion tests

A step is finished only when the evidence exists.

These four tests sit on top of the register. They are the questions a lead can ask in the daily sync without opening every SOP.

Work

Ticket and evidence

No work starts without a ticket. No ticket is complete without acceptance criteria and the required evidence.

Release

Journeys validated

The release closes when critical business journeys have been checked in production, after the deployment itself.

Recovery

Restore demonstrated

The recovery plan stands when a drill has restored the service and verified data integrity within the agreed RTO and RPO.

Configuration

Human authorization

AI-drafted formulas, underwriting rules, and customer documents are published only after validation and a named approval.

Release checklist

Run the list in order. Rollback and database recovery are decided before the window opens, including for an emergency hotfix, which still needs testing and a retrospective approval.

  1. 01Scope and version freeze
  2. 02Code freeze and merge approval
  3. 03QA sign-off and regression
  4. 04UAT sign-off where required
  5. 05Security and configuration checks
  6. 06Database migration and backup
  7. 07Deployment approval and window
  8. 08Execution by a named owner
  9. 09Smoke test and business validation
  10. 10Monitor, close, or roll back

Incident path

P&C runs this through the Response Center. Salik–LIVA and Motor use the same severity, ownership, and communication rules, with their own escalation contacts.

  1. 01 · DetectAPM, logs, uptime, API failures, and business-transaction alerts
  2. 02 · RecordTicket, logs, time, environment, correlation ID, and product
  3. 03 · ClassifySeverity, impact, module team, and an incident commander when major
  4. 04 · RecoverInvestigate, mitigate, communicate, fix, and validate
  5. 05 · PreventCause, corrective action, better monitoring, and a post-incident review

Incident record

  • Platform
  • Environment
  • Service
  • Customer or business impact
  • Severity
  • First detected time
  • Ticket owner
  • Incident timeline
  • Linked deployment
  • Root cause
  • Corrective action
  • Next update time

AI-assisted product configuration

Use AI to draft from an approved specification. Publication stays with a product owner or authorized underwriter. The same rule covers Motor data cleansing before a vehicle is rated.

  1. 01Draft fields and validation
  2. 02Draft formulas, rules, and documents
  3. 03Consistency checks
  4. 04Scenario validation
  5. 05Human approval
  6. 06Version, publish, and audit
  7. 07Monitor and roll back

08 · Management dashboard

One scorecard, three platforms.

Drill down by platform, module, sprint, and owner. Use the same definitions everywhere so a comparison between P&C, Salik–LIVA, and Motor means something. Set a baseline, then agree the target.

Delivery

Commit versus done. Sprint predictability, milestones, and release status.

Quality

Defect trends, escaped defects, reopen rate, and regression results.

Reliability

Availability, latency, incidents, and time to restore service.

Resources

Allocation, support load, blockers, leave, and module ownership.

Sprint completion ratePlanned work versus work actually completed.
Cycle timeTime from the start of work to completion.
Release frequencyHow often production releases occur.
Change failure rateDeployments that need a rollback, hotfix, or other remediation.
Defect escape rateDefects found after release, against all defects.
MTTRMean time to restore service after an incident.
SLA complianceIncidents handled inside the agreed response and restoration targets.
API success rateReliability of critical external integrations, including insurer APIs.
Reopened ticket rateA signal of gaps in implementation or verification.
Overdue action itemsDecisions, corrective actions, and commitments past their due date.

Judge contribution through quality, predictability, and collaboration. Account for complexity, support duty, review time, and dependencies.

09 · Monthly management pack

Nine source reports. Four packs leadership reads.

Teams keep the nine reports because each one has a different owner and evidence trail. The management review reads four packs so business performance, delivery, reliability, and people stay distinct.

Pack 1 · SOP-001

Business transaction metrics

What volume did each platform process, how did it move, and where did conversion or transactions fail?

Pack 2 · SOP-002, 006, 008

Delivery & release

What reached production, what reached UAT, what is still pending, and what is committed next month?

Pack 3 · SOP-003, 005, 007, 009

Technology & reliability

How are predictability, quality, deployments, incidents, API reliability, and recovery trending?

Pack 4 · SOP-004, 006

Team & next month

What did each person and module deliver, and what are the sprint commitments for next month?

Inside the business transaction report

Publish each platform on its own, then a consolidated view. Define the unit before the number is used: unique quotes apart from quote versions, API attempts apart from successful business transactions, and issued policies apart from renewals or endorsements.

Transaction volume

  • Initiated and completed
  • Quotes and policies issued
  • Premium or transaction value
  • By product, insurer, broker, or channel

Conversion

  • Leads, quotes, and policies
  • Quote-to-policy conversion
  • Drop-off by journey stage
  • Time from quote to issue

Operational performance

  • Successful and failed transactions
  • Failure rate and top reasons
  • External API success and downtime
  • Pending items, retries, mismatches

Month on month

  • Current month against the last
  • Absolute change and growth
  • Product and platform trends
  • Business and technical reasons for a material move

10 · Enforceability

Same template. Named approver. An audit trail.

A process is in force when it has an owner, a place it lives, evidence someone can retrieve, and a consequence when the same gap repeats.

  1. 01
    SOP ID, title, and version
  2. 02
    Purpose and scope
  3. 03
    Applicable platforms and modules
  4. 04
    Process owner and responsible roles
  5. 05
    Trigger and required inputs
  6. 06
    Step-by-step procedure
  7. 07
    Mandatory checklist and approval gates
  8. 08
    Expected outputs and evidence
  9. 09
    SLA, deadlines, and escalation
  10. 10
    Exceptions and emergency procedure
  11. 11
    Tools, templates, and linked documents
  12. 12
    Audit trail, review frequency, and change history

Responsible

Does the work and produces the evidence.

Approver

Signs the output and accepts exceptions.

Consulted

Contributes before the decision is made.

Informed

Receives the outcome and any change in date.

  • Central repositoryVersion-controlled home for every SOP.
  • Master registerOwner and next review date for each SOP.
  • AcknowledgmentTraining or sign-off for the people the SOP applies to.
  • Compliance auditPeriodic check of evidence against the procedure.
  • Exception registerApproved deviations, with an expiry and an approver.
  • Corrective actionRaised when the same non-compliance shows up again.

11 · Rollout

Four phases, about six weeks.

Introduce the operating rhythm and the quality gates before the full control set. Week 6 publishes the handbook against evidence from the first five weeks, including the gaps still open.

Week 1

Foundation

  • Appoint process owners
  • Standardize the master tracker
  • Agree ticket statuses
  • Start the daily lead sync
  • Define the monthly pack

Weeks 2–3

Engineering quality

  • Branching and pull request rules
  • QA entry and exit criteria
  • Regression for critical journeys
  • Requirement templates
  • Release checklist

Weeks 4–5

Reliability & security

  • Alert routing
  • Support ticket automation
  • Incident SLAs
  • Security gates
  • Backup verification
  • Recovery runbooks

Week 6

Governance & audit

  • Review compliance evidence
  • Measure the first KPI baseline
  • Record gaps and exceptions
  • Refine the SOPs that failed the audit
  • Publish the operating handbook
